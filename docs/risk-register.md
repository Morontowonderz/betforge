## Operational response policy — DESIGN

Before an affected activity, check all applicable OPEN and BLOCKED entries. BLOCKED entries are hard stops for the named activity, not for independent reversible documentation work. OPEN does not mean safe or accepted: identify applicable gates and controls before proceeding. Legal, security and data-rights blockers override numeric readiness.

If a critical data/identity/clock/control check fails, record the failure and produce no signal; never silently substitute a quote or widen the acceptance threshold. If a tool/test fails, preserve the exact error, state a hypothesis, apply one scoped minimal change and rerun. After two failures on the same cause, stop that subsystem, preserve evidence and escalate; independent work may continue.

Do not place wagers, send external notifications, publish, purchase or change production as a mitigation. Those actions require discrete approval. Do not rotate existing credentials as an automatic incident response; escalate suspected exposure without printing values, stop the affected integration, and request the owner's credential-response decision.

## Closure and review protocol — DESIGN

1. Assign a named owner before the risk-bearing activity, with deadline tied to that activity's gate rather than an invented date.
2. Attach evidence paths, versions, source terms dates and executed test results. A proposed control is not closure evidence.
3. The specified independent verifier checks the evidence and records residual risk. The coordinator applies any status transition; no agent self-certifies closure.
4. The owner may accept documented residual operational risk, but cannot use acceptance to waive legal/data permissions or explicit approval requirements.
5. Reopen after provider terms, markets, model/data versions, jurisdictions or delivery channels change; review again before each phase transition. No unattended review schedule is created.
